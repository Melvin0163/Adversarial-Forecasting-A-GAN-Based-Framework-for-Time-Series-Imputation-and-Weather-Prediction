\begin{abstract}
Prolonged exposure to extreme heat is an increasingly recognised danger, and heatwaves have become a serious threat to all living beings. Their analysis depends on long, continuous temperature records, which are often incomplete. We propose a generative adversarial network (GAN) based framework for heatwave-oriented temperature imputation and forecasting over the Chennai region. Daily climate data for the period 1950 to 2025 were collected through API-based retrieval from Google Earth Engine (GEE). After exploratory data analysis and the definition of a heatwave threshold, three GAN models were developed and evaluated: a conventional GAN, a Shared GAN in which a single generator performs both imputation and forecasting, and a Shared Wasserstein GAN with Gradient Penalty (Shared WGAN-GP). The models were trained, validated and tested on a chronological split with simulated random and multi-day gaps, and their performance was comparatively assessed for missing-data imputation and short-range temperature forecasting, focusing on extreme heatwave events. Among the evaluated architectures, the Shared WGAN-GP demonstrated comparatively superior performance in terms of the evaluation metrics, indicating its potential for robust temporal data imputation and heatwave forecasting. Future work will advance these imputations and forecasts towards El Ni\~no-aware modelling by incorporating El Ni\~no--Southern Oscillation indices, in order to capture the influence of large-scale climate variability on regional heatwaves.
\end{abstract}
